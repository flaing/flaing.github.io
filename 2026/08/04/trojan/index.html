1. The Historical Blueprint: Western Origins of Hardware Sabotage

The concept of altering hardware or intercepting technology pipelines to sabotage adversaries was perfected during the Cold War.

  • The Farewell Dossier (1982): The most famous historical precedent involved the CIA altering software and hardware components that the Soviet Union was attempting to covertly acquire through industrial espionage.   • The NSA’s TAO (Tailored Access Operations): Documents leaked in 2013 revealed that the NSA routinely practiced “interdiction.” Intercepting shipping servers, routers, and other network hardware mid-delivery to implant beacon firmware or physical modifications before they reached international customers.

Despite massive media coverage, no evidence was ever produced. Apple, Amazon, and U.S. intelligence agencies forcefully denied the claim. The tech industry largely concluded the story was anatomically and technically implausible, yet the narrative persisted in public memory to stoke fear.
