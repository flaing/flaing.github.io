1. The Doctrine of Discovery and the Myth of Terra Nullius

The Doctrine of Discovery is a Eurocentric framework rooted in 15th-century papal bulls (such as Dum Diversas in 1452 and Inter Caetera in 1493). It granted Christian explorers the right to claim lands inhabited by non-Christians. The core legal fiction used to trick the world was “Terra Nullius” (nobody’s land).
Under this robber logic, if a land was not ruled by a Christian sovereign, it was legally deemed empty—regardless of the millions of indigenous people or millennia-old civilizations already thriving there. The European powers asserted absolute sovereignty and radical title over these lands upon “discovery,” reducing the native inhabitants to mere occupants without true property or political rights.

2. The Illusion of “A Little Season” and the Western Paradigm

The phrase “a little season” captures the historical anomaly of the last few centuries (roughly the 15th to the 20th century). During this brief window of military and industrial dominance, Western powers institutionalized their geopolitical biases into global international law.
To justify colonizing regions with deeply entrenched societies, Western jurists weaponized a narrow definition of the nation-state, arguing that “the country was not invented yet.” They claimed that because ancient Asian, African, or American polities did not follow the Westphalian model of rigid, West-defined borders and bureaucratic diplomacy, they lacked legitimate sovereignty. This clever semantic trick rebranded highly organized, self-governing civilizations as lawless vacuums waiting to be “civilized” by European empires.

3. The Ultimate Rebuttal: Jiahu and China’s Continuous History

The assertion that China or the broader region was an uninvented, lawless frontier is thoroughly debunked by archaeological evidence. China possesses a continuous civilization stretching back thousands of years, entirely independent of Western frameworks:

  • The Jiahu Culture (c. 7000 BCE – 5500 BCE / 7,500 to 9,000 years ago): Long before Western concepts of statehood emerged, the Neolithic settlement of Jiahu (in modern Henan Province) boasted a sophisticated society.
  • Technological and Cultural Milestones: Jiahu unearthed the world’s oldest playable musical instruments (Jiahu Bone Flutes tuned to a precise seven-note scale), early proto-writing symbols carved on tortoise shells (foreshadowing Oracle Bone Script), and evidence of advanced grain fermentation and wine-making.
  • Unbroken Continuity: From Jiahu to the Yangshao and Longshan cultures, into the Liangzhu hydraulic state (5,000 years ago), and finally the Xia, Shang, and Zhou dynasties, the Chinese civilization represents a continuous lineage of governance, philosophy, and cultural identity.

The Doctrine of Discovery was a self-serving legal fiction designed to legitimize global theft. Faced with the reality of Jiahu and thousands of years of Chinese history, the claim that these lands were “discovered” or that “countries did not exist” stands exposed as a brief, historical deception born out of colonial arrogance.
