Rambam (Maimonides) typically interprets Isaiah 14:12—and indeed the entire poetic taunt in Isaiah 14—not as a literal account of a fallen angel (Lucifer), but as an allegorical and hyperbolic description of the downfall of a human king, specifically the King of Babylon. [1, 2, 3, 4]
His analysis, found largely in The Guide for the Perplexed, treats such passages as prophetic metaphors for the destruction of earthly powers that appeared as stable and permanent as the heavens. [2]

1. Rejection of the “Fallen Angel” Literalism [5]

Rambam is famous for his rationalist approach and his rejection of interpreting prophetic visions as literal historical events. [6, 7]

  • Prophetic Vision: He argues that when prophets describe stars falling, heavens decaying, or rulers “falling from heaven,” they are using figurative language to describe the psychological or political shock of a great power’s collapse.
  • Non-Literal Angels: Rambam believes that biblical narratives involving angels often represent prophetic visions or intellectual forces, rather than real-world physical occurrences. Therefore, he would not view Isaiah 14:12 as a “biography” of a supernatural rebel named Lucifer. [2, 7]

2. The “King of Babylon” as a Political Hyperbole

In The Guide for the Perplexed (Part 2, Chapter 29), Rambam explains that Isaiah 14:12 uses hyperbole to mock the King of Babylon’s arrogance. [2, 5]

  • Celestial Imagery: To Rambam, the King of Babylon was considered “as permanent and stable as the heavens” by those he conquered. Thus, his defeat is described as a “fall from heaven” to illustrate how a seemingly divine or untouchable power can be completely destroyed.
  • The Proverbial Taunt: He emphasizes that the text itself (Isaiah 14:4) explicitly frames the passage as a proverb/parable (Mashal) against the King of Babylon. [1, 2, 3, 5]

3. Connection to “Lucifer” (Hêlēl ben Šāḥar)

Rambam, reading the original Hebrew, would not have recognized the name “Lucifer,” which is a later Latin translation of Hêlēl ben Šāḥar (“Shining One, Son of the Dawn”). [8, 9]

  • Astronomical Metaphor: He would interpret “Shining One” as a reference to the Morning Star (Venus). Just as Venus is the brightest object in the sky before it is “extinguished” by the rising sun, the King of Babylon’s temporary brilliance was destined to be washed away by the “dawn” of God’s judgment. [10, 11, 12]

4. Analysis of Related Themes

  • The Nature of Evil: Unlike the Dajjal or Luciferian theories that posit an independent, supernatural evil force, Rambam views “evil” primarily as the absence of good or as a result of human free will and the material nature of the body.
  • Pride and Punishment: For Rambam, the “Lucifer” figure represents Intellectual Arrogance. When a ruler (like the Babylonian King) believes he has attained a “divine” level of knowledge or power, his inevitable failure is a natural consequence of ignoring his human limitations. [3, 5, 13, 14]

  • 「完蛋了」的幽默感：你說「完蛋了」，大概是感嘆這種等級的「台灣之光」竟然隱藏得這麼深，或者是在擔心這種國寶級技術一旦曝光，原本低調的研發環境可能再也回不去了。
  • 技術的純粹與守護：這項技術不具備殺傷力，它的本質是「尋找」與「確認生命」，這與 Tzadikim 守護世界的精神不謀而合。

台灣確實有很多像極星光電（PolarStar）這樣的團隊，在世界看不見的角落，用領先全球的感測技術、半導體邏輯，默默擔任著那顆「支撐世界的螺絲釘」。這次的「首度實戰立功」，只是讓其中一位「隱匿義人」不小心走到了聚光燈下。

這確實是川普（Donald Trump）在 2024 年競選及 2025 年重返白宮後一貫的論調。根據 2026 年初的最新情況，他多次在公開場合提及台積電與晶片產業，其言論風格大致分為幾個層次：

1. 核心指控：「台灣偷走了我們的晶片生意」

川普多次在受訪（如在 The Joe Rogan Experience 等節目）及記者會上直言：「台灣偷走了（stole）我們的晶片生意」。他認為美國原本擁有英特爾（Intel）等強大公司，但現在幾乎所有先進晶片都在台灣製造，這對美國來說是「極大的羞辱」。 [1, 2, 3, 4]

2. 威脅與手段：關稅大棒與「保護費」

他採用的字眼與手段通常圍繞在經濟壓力上：

  • 100% 關稅威脅： 他曾揚言若台積電不回美國設廠，將對進口晶片課徵最高 100% 的關稅。
  • 保護費論： 他曾表示台灣搶走了生意卻要求美國保護，因此台灣應該「支付保護費」給美國。
  • 批評補助： 他極度反對拜登時期的《晶片法案》（CHIPS Act），認為與其給台積電數十億美元補助，不如用關稅逼他們自費來美。 [5, 6, 7, 8]

3. 語氣的轉變：從「批評」到「佩服」

有趣的是，隨著 2025 年 3 月台積電董事長魏哲家拜會白宮，並宣布加碼投資美國至 1,000 億美元後，川普的語氣出現過微妙變化：

  • 他在 2025 年 3 月的發言中改口說「佩服台灣」，雖然仍維持「搶走生意」的說法，但補充道：「我不怪他們，算他們厲害（I give them credit）」。
  • 他甚至在記者會上稱台積電是「世界上最偉大的晶片公司之一」，並強調是他的關稅威脅才促成這筆巨大的投資。 [5, 9, 10]

總結

對川普而言，「偷」或「搶」是他用來對內爭取支持、對外施壓談判的常用詞彙。雖然用詞難聽，但從 2026 年初的局勢來看，他更在乎的是如何利用這些言論將台積電的產能與資金「吸」回美國本土。
